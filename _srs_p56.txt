Accounts, Finance and GST
Purpose
Records the money side of every transaction automatically, so that the books are always in step with the
stock and the sales — and produces the statutory outputs Indian manufacturers must file.
How it works
The system uses double-entry accounting with a configurable chart of accounts. Documents created in
other modules post their accounting entries automatically through a posting rule map: a sales invoice
debits the customer and credits sales and GST output; a GRN debits inventory and credits goods-received-
not-invoiced; a supplier bill clears that liability and credits the supplier. Users never type these entries —
they are generated, which is what keeps stock value and the general ledger in agreement.
On top of the automatic postings, users record payments, receipts, journal entries, contra entries and
expenses. Receipts are allocated against specific invoices, so the outstanding report is by invoice and by
age,  not  just  a  customer  total.  GST  output  is  prepared  as  working  sheets  for  GSTR-1  and  GSTR-3B,
reconciled against GSTR-2B data imported from the portal, and exported in the required format.

User stories
US-M13-01 — As an Accountant, I want invoices to post to the ledger automatically, so that I do not enter
the same transaction twice.
Acceptance: confirming an invoice creates a balanced journal entry visible from the invoice screen; deleting is
impossible and reversal is only through a credit note.
US-M13-02 — As an Accountant, I want to allocate a single receipt of ₹5,00,000 across four invoices, so that
ageing is correct.
Acceptance: the receipt screen lists open invoices oldest first with an auto-allocate button; the allocated total
cannot exceed the receipt amount; any unallocated balance stays as an advance against the customer.
