5. Access Control — RBAC and Authentication
5.1 The model in plain words
A user is a person who logs in. A role is a job title such as Store Keeper. A permission is one specific thing
that can be done, such as "approve a purchase order". Permissions are given to roles, roles are given to
users. A user may hold more than one role, and may also be given or denied a single permission directly,
which overrides the role.
On top of this the system adds two more layers that most small ERPs miss:
Record scope — which records the user may see. A sales executive with the "view sales order"
permission still only sees orders of customers assigned to them.
Field rules — which columns the user may see or edit. A production planner may open a sales order
but must not see the selling price.

5.2 Permission naming convention
Every permission is named module.action in lower snake case. This is not cosmetic — the settings screen
groups permissions automatically by the part before the dot, and the seeder generates them from a single
array.
Action suffix Example Meaning
.view sales_order.view Open the list and the detail screen (read only)
.create sales_order.create Create a new draft record
.update sales_order.update Edit a record that is still editable in its current status
.delete sales_order.delete Soft delete a draft record
.approve sales_order.approve Move a record from Pending Approval to Approved
.cancel sales_order.cancel Cancel a confirmed record, with a mandatory reason
.print sales_order.print Generate the PDF
.export sales_order.export Download Excel or CSV — treated as a sensitive
permission and always logged

EVERY REQUEST PASSES THROUGH FOUR GATES
1. Is the user logged in and active? → 2. Does the user hold the permission? → 3. Is this record inside the user's scope? → 4. Are the submitted fields editable by this role?
Failing gate 2, 3 or 4 returns HTTP 403 and writes an entry to the security log.
